# Writing realistic dialogue

Rules for how people text in the Story, above all for the dates in Part 1. The aim is texts that read like a real person sent them, not like an AI wrote them. Not everyone is supportive, and not every chat ends well.

## Reactions
- People react the way they would in real life. A date who works out he's gay can be annoyed, embarrassed, put off, feel misled or feel she wasted her night, and she can say so.
- Don't soften every ending. Most dates end flat ("ok", "yeah", "bye"), cold, or left on read. Keep a kind line for the odd chat where it fits that person; it shouldn't be the default.
- No redemption arcs in a single chat. The person who was put off doesn't come round three messages later with a compliment.
- People are self-interested. They care about their own night, their parking, their friends seeing, how it made them look ("i felt like an idiot", "my sister's friend was two tables over").
- Each person reacts in their own way: Hannah is blunt and short on time, Mia avoids conflict, Ella feels blindsided, Tayla is embarrassed in front of her friends, Georgie is direct. Don't give two people the same line.

## How real people text
- Short messages, often several in a row. Lowercase, little punctuation, dropped apostrophes ("dont", "im", "thats"), and "lol", "tbh", "ngl" used the way people actually use them.
- Plain words. People say "it was weird", "i'm not into it", "you should've said". They don't explain their feelings in full sentences.
- No therapy-speak or AI tells: no "I appreciate you sharing", "that's valid", "for what it's worth", "I mean that genuinely", "honestly, I respect that", no neat closing reflections, no witty one-liner for every message.
- People don't answer everything. They dodge, repeat themselves, get the last word, or stop replying.
- Specific details beat general ones: $18 parking, the lady with the pram, the waiter's arms, "your pics are literally you in a polo shirt at a wedding".

## In-person scenes
- Chapters with `irl: true` are spoken, not texted. People talk in full sentences and can say a few lines at a time, but it's still plain speech: "I'm not angry. I'm just tired of pretending I can't see it."
- Use `{ note }` lines for what she does (puts her coffee down, looks at his toes, goes quiet). Keep them short and physical, never what she's thinking.
- Break-ups give him lots of different reactions: denial, crying, begging for another chance, getting angry, joking, going quiet, admitting it, asking to stay friends. Each girl answers each one in her own way, and she doesn't change her mind.

## Encouragement and losing interest
- Some girls (Bianca most of all) encourage him to go gayer the more they suspect: a tinted gloss, a colour on his nails, a skirt, lashes, heels. Their attraction drops as their suspicion rises, and the way they talk changes with it: flirty, then "babe" in a friend way, then fully one of the girls.
- The encouragement is genuine hype, never a joke at his expense. Losing interest shows in small physical things (a shorter hug, she reaches for his hand only to look at his nails, she turns her cheek).

## Progression within a route
- Each girl's route gets gayer chapter by chapter, in what he wears (outfit picks scale up too) and how he talks. Date one he's fairly blokey and awkward. By the third date he says "babe", "obsessed", "it's giving", and knows brands. By the end chapter he's fully camp, while still saying he's straight.
- Her attraction drops and her suspicion rises in step (the bars follow the chapter's `gaydar`).
- Girls write longer now: several messages a turn with real detail. His replies are full sentences too, not one-word buttons.

## His replies
- Keep his options human too: awkward, defensive, a bit snarky, apologetic, or honest. They aren't speeches and they don't fix the situation.
- Every reply is still a fine reply. No scoring.

## Letting him down
- Once a girl stops fancying him she says so the way people really do: offhand, a bit mocking, not a speech. "you'd have more luck with the waiter lol", "like youd be a great boyfriend honestly. for a guy".
- Teasing him towards guys is fine and usually lands in the break-up or end chapter: she points out who he was looking at, says he should be on the guy apps, or has a friend lined up for him.
- Each girl does it her own way (Hannah blunt, Mia apologising, Georgie counting things, Lucy in her nurse voice, Zoe through Brooke). Don't give two girls the same line, and don't lean on stock beats (head pats, "one of the girls", "puppy cute", fixing-things comparisons) more than once a route.
- In the barefoot chapters she teases him about his small feet (smaller than hers, a size six, "princess feet"). One line per chapter, different every time.
- On the boy-clothes path the same girls are into him while he keeps it up; when he slips they notice and say it in the same teasing voice.

## Where the line is
These rules sit inside the content boundaries in CLAUDE.md:
- Annoyed, blunt, teasing and disappointed is fine. Slurs, cruelty for its own sake, or a scene built around humiliating him for being gay or feminine are not.
- Embarrassing moments are fine when they're gentle and the people around him stay warm (his painted toes showing when Mia makes him take his socks off, going red at the new boyfriend). Nobody laughs at him to hurt him.
- The guys who want the girls (and the new boyfriends when he tries to win her back) tease him and make it obvious they don't see him as a threat. Ribbing, smirks and pet names are fine. No slurs, no threats, nothing physical beyond a hair ruffle, a pat on the cheek, or (in the barefoot chapters) grabbing his ankle to tickle his feet, and in the ambush chapters pulling his shoes and socks off while he protests, with the girls holding him down and joining in. Tickling is playful and non-sexual, and it stops the moment he says stop. When he can't say it, she (or the guy) calls it.
- The focus stays on him and how he handles it. The narrator `after` note and the `debrief` carry the warmth, so the dialogue doesn't have to.
- Jess, Nicole and {bf} are allowed to be warm. Warm still means real people talking: specific memories and normal texting, not polished paragraphs.

## Check before pushing
Read each line aloud and ask: would someone actually send this? If it sounds like a greeting card, a counsellor or a sitcom, rewrite it.

## The girls' voices
The girls have a valley-girl lilt ("like", "literally", "oh my god", "wait", "no because", "obsessed"), used unevenly the way people really talk, never one per sentence. Each has her own flavour: Archie tired and passive-aggressive ("sooo", "whatever"), Hannah fast and blunt with anatomy words, Mia apologising and trailing off, Ella theatrical ("I'm dying"), Tayla group-chat brain with one word in caps, Georgie counting and ranking, Lucy clinical night-shift nurse ("bless"), Jade half-asleep ("lowkey", "vibes", only her), Olivia rehearsing how she'll tell her sister, Zoe quoting Brooke, Sophie posh-ish ("totes", "stop", "I'm screaming"), Bianca hype ("BABE", "yesss"). Not "mother" (it trips the family-word check).

Inner thoughts (`thinks`) are messy: they jump around, go petty, end mid-way or on a tangent. Avoid AI tells: neat punchline closers, "Not X. Y.", rule-of-three lists, similes, wise conclusions, "genuinely", "main character", "core memory", "the audacity", em dashes.

## Confrontations and tears
The big chapters happen in front of her friends, and no two come out the same way. Each has a path where the embarrassment gets too much and he cries. The moment he does, nobody laughs at him: the girls look after him (a hoodie, tissues, a drink, fixing his mascara, sitting with him) and from then on he's one of the girls, not a boyfriend. Each tears scene tips over and gets comforted differently, and the chapter ends with a plain `debrief`.
