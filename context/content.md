# Content (`content.js`)

## Placeholders
`{name}`: his name (Settings, default "Calum"). `{bf}`: the boyfriend's name (Settings, default "Matt"). Always render text through `fill()`.

## Shape
```js
window.CONTENT = {
  routes: [Route],                 // the list of girls (and Jess, {bf})
  story: [Chapter]                 // in order; each has a route
}
Route    = { id, name, blurb, part?, lock?, avatar?, intro? }
Chapter  = { id, route, title, contact?, intro?, irl?, friend?, gaydarSays?, gaydarWho?,
             outfits?, outfitPrompt?, ...Node }   // contact defaults to {bf}
Node     = { them: [Msg], replies?: [{ you, ...Node }], after?, thinks?, debrief?, gaydar?, spark?, outcome? }
Msg      = string | { from, text } | { note } | { text, if } | { note, if }   // from = sender in group chats; note = narration (what she does), mainly for irl scenes
```
- IDs must be unique and never change: progress is stored by id.
- Every reply is a fine reply. There's no wrong answer and no scoring.
- A reply with no `them` and no `replies` is a leaf: it just ends the chapter.
- `irl: true` marks an in-person scene (spoken, with `{ note }` narration). `friend: true` hides the attraction bar.
- `after` is a narrator note shown when the conversation ends (the deepest branch's own takes priority). A chapter-level `after` shows on every branch that doesn't have its own, so it must be true whichever way the chat went: put branch-specific notes (he tried to win her back, he gave Riley a pedicure) on that branch. `debrief` is a short "worth remembering" note.
- `outcome` on a branch records how the chapter ended (deepest branch wins; replaying overwrites it). A later message with `if: "chapterId=a|b"` only shows when that chapter last ended with one of those outcomes. The tickle chapters use `held`, `cracked`, `begged` and `kept` (let him keep going).
- `thinks` (array of short paragraphs) is what the girl was really thinking on an in-person date, shown at the end in an "In {girl}'s head" card above the bars. Like `after`, the deepest branch's own wins, so the chapter's must be true on every branch and outfit; put what only fits one branch on that branch. An outfit's `thinks` (one string) is her first look at what he wore, shown before the rest. First person, past tense, her own voice: honest and a bit unfiltered (what she noticed, what she didn't say, who she's going to tell), not cruel, no therapy-speak. It shouldn't repeat `after` or `gaydarSays`.
- `gaydar` (0–100) on a date chapter shows a "How gay {girl} thinks you are" bar when it ends. Set a base on the chapter and raise it on the branches where he says it (100 = he told her). The deepest branch that sets it wins. A second bar, "How into you {girl} is", shows `spark` (deepest branch wins) or, by default, 100 minus the gaydar: the more she suspects, the less she fancies him. `gaydarSays` is her teasing one-liner under the bars; it shows for every branch, so in the tickle chapters it mustn't assume he laughed (he can hold it in). `gaydarWho` names the girl when `contact` is a group chat (c6c: Tayla). Labels come from `gaydarLabel()` in index.html.

## Story
Story is split into **routes** (`routes: [{ id, name, blurb, part, lock? }]`), and every chapter has a `route`. Chapters in a route open one after another as soon as the previous one is finished. Routes with `lock` (Jess, {bf}) open once any other route is finished.
- **Archie** (c1, c1b, archie-feet, c3, c4): three years together. She works out he's gay, leaves him for Josh from logistics (pickup soccer, three polo shirts), then tries to set him up with Josh's friend Ryan (reformer pilates, a dog called Margot). He never comes out to her.
- **Nicole** (c2, nicole-2, nicole-3): she never asks and he never tells her; she just starts treating him as one of the girls (girls' night, Sephora, rating her Hinge guys). `friend: true` hides the attraction bar.
- **The girls** (Hannah, Mia, Ella, Tayla, Georgie, Lucy, Jade, Olivia, Zoe, Sophie, Bianca): seven to ten chapters each.

  See [routes.md](routes.md) for every route's chapters in order, including the extra dates and the boy-clothes path.

  Date one is low-key; each date he's a bit gayer and more femme in how he dresses and how he talks; the big chapter (the older c5–c6m ids) is where she says it or breaks up with him; the last (`<name>-end`) is her with a straight guy who doesn't crossdress (Brodie the electrician, Lachlan the accountant, Connor the rugby player, Mitch who plays football, Ben who does rope access, Hamish the ag consultant, Riley the plumber, James in finance, Kyle the mechanic, Nathan the physio, Liam the carpenter).
- **Trying to win her back:** every end chapter has a branch where he goes up to the new guy to say he wants her back. He comes across very meek and obviously gay (says sorry, talks in a small voice, holds his drink with both hands), the guy gives him butterflies (he loses his words, goes red, compliments his arms or his shirt), and the guy knows he's no threat and teases him for it: cocky, laughing, calling him "champ", "princess" or "sweetheart", offering him something pink with a little straw, announcing it to his friends. She says no but thinks he's adorable. In `mia-end` he turns up at her flat and she makes him take his shoes and socks off at the door (new floors), which shows his glittery lilac toes in front of Lachlan. Nobody is cruel to him.

## Voice
Warm and casual. It's set in Brisbane (James St, the Valley, West End, South Bank, New Farm, Paddington), but people talk in plain, neutral English: no Aussie slang ("mate", "g'day", "arvo", "heaps", "reckon", "thongs", "footy", "snag", "sunnies"), and no local brands or footy teams (no Bunnings, Myer, Kmart, Sportsgirl or Holden; use Zara, H&M, "the hardware store", "a rugby jersey"). Places are fine (the Wickham, GOMA, Howard Smith Wharves). Guys say "man" or "buddy". The girls' chats can run long: several messages per turn, real paragraphs about how they feel. Other chats stay short. Use emoji sparingly. Use `{name}` naturally, especially from the girls. See [dialogue.md](dialogue.md) for how people text (realistic, not always nice) and CLAUDE.md for content boundaries.

## Outfit picks
An outfit can also have `masc: true` (boy clothes, counts towards the boy-clothes path) and `spark` (a delta on the "fancies you" bar). A reply can have `masc: true` too.
A date chapter can have `outfits: [{ label, intro?, gaydar?, them?, at? }]` (usually toned down / the usual / further). The pick replaces the chapter `intro`, inserts `them` lines (her reaction) after her first line (or at index `at`), and adds `gaydar` (a delta) to the final value, capped at 99 unless a branch reached 100. Every option must keep whatever the dialogue mentions (e.g. Jade's heeled sandals), so options add or soften around it rather than removing it. `outfitPrompt` overrides "What are you wearing?". The same goes for the end chapters' dialogue and `after`: if the toned-down option is jeans and lip oil, nobody can mention his lashes, heels or painted toes unless every option has them.

## Validator
`node test/validate-content.cjs` (plain Node, no dependencies) loads content.js with a fake `window` and checks it against this file. Run it after every content change; it exits 1 on errors.
- **Errors:** unique, non-empty route and chapter ids; every chapter's route exists and every route has chapters; only known keys on routes, chapters, branches, messages and outfits; every chapter has `them`, every reply has `you`, no empty `them`/`replies`; message objects have exactly one of `text`/`note`; `gaydar`/`spark` are 0–100; outfits are well-formed (`label`, delta `gaydar`, `at` in range); `if` conditions name an existing chapter and only outcomes some branch of it sets; only `{name}`/`{bf}` placeholders and no stray braces; no empty strings; none of the banned slang above, and no family members (Mum, Nan, Dad, parents) as whole words.
- **Warnings:** local brands and footy teams, the therapy-speak phrases from dialogue.md, the narrator patterns above, double spaces, stray whitespace, doubled punctuation, repeated words, common misspellings, unbalanced brackets or quotes, a message identical to the one before it (same sender), sibling replies with the same text, an `if` pointing at a later chapter.

The word lists live at the top of the script: keep them in sync with the Voice section. It can't judge tone, so still read new lines aloud (dialogue.md).
